1The rule

Training set \((x_i, y_i)\), \(i = 1, \dots, m\). For a query \(x\), \(N_k(x)\) = indices of the \(k\) training points closest to \(x\).

TaskPrediction at \(x\)
Classification
majority vote		\(\hat P(Y = c \mid X = x) = \frac1k \sum_{i \in N_k(x)} \mathbf{1}\{y_i = c\}, \quad \hat y(x) = \arg\max_c \hat P(Y = c \mid X = x)\)
Regression
local average		\(\hat f(x) = \frac1k \sum_{i \in N_k(x)} y_i\)
  • Local estimates of \(P(Y = c \mid X = x)\) and of \(\mathbb{E}[Y \mid X = x]\): class proportions, or mean response, among the neighbours.
  • No training. The model is the stored dataset. Cost at prediction: \(m\) distances in \(\mathbb{R}^d\) per query.
  • Distance. Euclidean, \(\|x - x_i\|^2 = \sum_j (x_j - x_{ij})^2\). Features on different scales must be standardised.

Regression, \(d = 1\). Drag the black handle to move \(x\). Dashed: true \(f(x) = \sin 2\pi x\). Shaded: the neighbours. Blue segment and dot: their mean \(\hat f(x)\). Faint curve: \(\hat f\) at every \(x\).

2The role of \(k\)

  • Small \(k\): very local, irregular boundary or \(\hat f\). Low bias, high variance.
  • Large \(k\): wide neighbourhoods, smooth boundary or \(\hat f\). High bias, low variance. \(k = m\): the global majority class (or the mean \(\bar y\)) everywhere.
  • Complexity \(\approx m/k\) effective parameters (number of disjoint neighbourhoods).
  • \(k\) is a hyperparameter: choose it on validation data, never on the training error.
Colour = predicted class, intensity = \(\hat P\) of that class. Accuracies: training points, and 1000 fresh test points from the same distribution.
