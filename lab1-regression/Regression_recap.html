Regression step by step

Key ideas behind Parts 1 to 3. All figures are interactive.

1From observations to a matrix

Observations \((x_i, y_i)\), \(i = 1, \dots, m\), with \(x_i \in \mathbb{R}^d\), \(y_i \in \mathbb{R}\). Linear model:

$$y_i = \theta_0 + \theta_1 x_{i1} + \dots + \theta_d x_{id} + \varepsilon_i = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & x_i^\top \end{bmatrix} \theta + \varepsilon_i, \qquad \theta = (\theta_0, \dots, \theta_d)^\top .$$

Stack the \(m\) equations. Each \(x_i\) becomes a row, preceded by a 1 for the intercept:

$$y = X\theta + \varepsilon, \qquad X = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & x_1^\top \\ \vdots & \vdots \\ 1 & x_m^\top \end{bmatrix} \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times (d+1)} .$$
Click a row (or an observation) to see its equation. Here \(d = 3\).

2Least squares

$$\hat\theta = \arg\min_\theta J(\theta), \qquad J(\theta) = \|y - X\theta\|^2 = \sum_{i=1}^m \big(y_i - [1\ \ x_i^\top]\,\theta\big)^2 .$$
  • Convex quadratic. \(\nabla J(\theta) = -2X^\top(y - X\theta)\), \(\nabla^2 J = 2X^\top X \succeq 0\). A stationary point is a global minimum.
  • First-order condition = normal equations:
$$\nabla J(\hat\theta) = 0 \iff X^\top X\,\hat\theta = X^\top y \iff \hat\theta = (X^\top X)^{-1} X^\top y \ \ \text{if } X^\top X \text{ invertible}.$$
Left: data (drag the points). Squares: residuals² of your line, total area = \(J(\theta)\). Right: level sets of \(J\) in the plane \((\theta_0, \theta_1)\). Drag the purple point to move your line. Black cross: \(\hat\theta\), dashed line: OLS fit.

Goodness of fit. With \(\hat y_i = [1\ \ x_i^\top]\,\hat\theta\) and \(\bar y = \frac1m \sum_i y_i\):

$$R^2 = 1 - \frac{\sum_{i=1}^m (y_i - \hat y_i)^2}{\sum_{i=1}^m (y_i - \bar y)^2} \in [0, 1] \quad \text{(with intercept)}.$$

Numerator: error of the model. Denominator: error of the constant prediction \(\bar y\). \(R^2\) = share of the variance of \(y\) explained.

Other indicators. \(\mathrm{RSS} = \sum_i (y_i - \hat y_i)^2\), \(\mathrm{TSS} = \sum_i (y_i - \bar y)^2\), \(k\) = number of parameters. The training RSS is optimistic, so the criteria below penalise \(k\).

IndicatorFormulaMeaning
RMSE\(\sqrt{\mathrm{RSS}/m}\)typical error, in the units of \(y\)
MAE\(\frac1m \sum_i |y_i - \hat y_i|\)typical error, robust to outliers
Adjusted \(R^2\)\(1 - \frac{\mathrm{RSS}/(m-k)}{\mathrm{TSS}/(m-1)}\)increases only if a feature helps more than chance
Mallows \(C_p\)\(\mathrm{RSS}/\hat\sigma^2 - m + 2k\)unbiased estimate of the prediction error. Good model: \(C_p \approx k\)
AIC\(-2\log\mathcal{L} + 2k\)estimates out-of-sample fit. For prediction
BIC\(-2\log\mathcal{L} + k\log m\)heavier penalty, smaller models. Finds the true model as \(m \to \infty\)
Cross-validationerror on held-out foldsdirect estimate, any model, no assumption

\(\hat\sigma^2\): noise variance estimated on the largest model. Smaller \(C_p\), AIC, BIC is better.

3Logistic regression

Motivation. Now \(y_i \in \{0, 1\}\). Linear regression predicts \(x^\top\theta \in \mathbb{R}\), not a probability. Squash the score into \((0, 1)\):

$$P(Y = 1 \mid x) = \sigma(x^\top\theta), \qquad \sigma(z) = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-z}} .$$
Drag the points sideways. Click a point to switch its label. Grey: linear regression, leaves \([0, 1]\). Purple: logistic fit, a probability.

Statistical model. Given \(x_i\), the \(Y_i\) are independent and

$$Y_i \mid x_i \sim \mathrm{Bernoulli}(p_i), \qquad p_i = \sigma(x_i^\top\theta), \qquad \mathcal{L}(\theta) = \prod_{i=1}^m p_i^{y_i} (1 - p_i)^{1 - y_i} .$$

Maximum likelihood = minimum of the cross-entropy \(-\frac1m \log \mathcal{L}\):

$$J(\theta) = -\frac1m \sum_{i=1}^m \Big[ y_i \log p_i + (1 - y_i) \log(1 - p_i) \Big].$$

4Softmax regression

\(K\) classes. One score per class, \(z_k = x^\top\theta_k\), turned into probabilities by the softmax:

$$Y_i \mid x_i \sim \mathrm{Categorical}(p_{i0}, \dots, p_{i,K-1}), \qquad p_{ik} = \frac{e^{z_{ik}}}{\sum_{j} e^{z_{ij}}} .$$
  • Positive, sum to 1, same order as the scores.
  • Boundaries \(z_k = z_l\) are linear in \(x\). Long \(\theta_k\) = confident predictions.
Drag the tips of \(\theta_0, \theta_1, \theta_2\) and the white point \(x\). Colour = most likely class, intensity = its probability. No intercept here.

Negative log-likelihood with one-hot labels \(Y_{ik} = \mathbf{1}\{y_i = k\}\). Only the true class counts:

$$J(\Theta) = -\frac1m \sum_{i=1}^m \sum_{k} Y_{ik} \log p_{ik} = -\frac1m \sum_{i=1}^m \log p_{i, y_i}, \qquad \nabla_\Theta J = \frac1m X^\top (P - Y).$$

No closed form: gradient descent.

5Ridge

Motivation. The OLS solution needs an inverse:

$$\hat\theta = (X^\top X)^{-1} X^\top y .$$
  • \(d > m\) or collinear features: \(X^\top X\) singular, no unique solution.
  • Correlated features: \(X^\top X\) nearly singular. Small eigenvalues \(\mu_j\) become large \(1/\mu_j\): \(\hat\theta\) explodes and changes a lot with the noise.

Fix. Penalise the size of \(\theta\):

$$\hat\theta_\lambda = \arg\min_\theta \ \|y - X\theta\|^2 + \lambda \|\theta\|^2, \qquad \lambda > 0 .$$
  • For \(\lambda > 0\) the problem is strictly convex: unique minimiser, even when \(X^\top X\) is singular.
  • \(\lambda \to 0\): OLS. \(\lambda \to \infty\): \(\theta \to 0\). Trade-off fit / stability, chosen on validation data.
